# CQC Audit Framework

Use this reference for every full or focused CQC audit.

## Live Source Check

CQC assessment methodology is moving during 2026. Before finalising an audit, check current official CQC pages and record the retrieval date. At minimum check:

- CQC assessment framework and five key questions.
- Current quality statements or sector-specific framework updates.
- CQC provider guidance relevant to the audited domain.
- CQC State of Care and provider bulletins where relevant.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014.
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009 notification duties.

If CQC has moved from the Single Assessment Framework to a sector-specific framework for this service type, use the current framework and explain the change in the methodology section. The audit should still preserve the five key questions because these remain the organising inspection language unless CQC explicitly replaces them.

## CQC Audit Spine

Assess all applicable evidence against:

| Key question | Core audit question |
|---|---|
| Safe | Are people protected from avoidable harm, abuse, poor infection control, unsafe medicines, unsafe premises/equipment, and foreseeable clinical or operational risk? |
| Effective | Is care evidence-based, clinically appropriate, outcome-monitored, and delivered by competent staff? |
| Caring | Are people treated with dignity, respect, kindness, privacy, autonomy, and meaningful involvement? |
| Responsive | Does the clinic respond to individual needs, communication needs, complaints, feedback, access barriers, and changing risks? |
| Well-Led | Are governance, audit, learning, leadership, risk management, records, culture, and improvement systems effective and evidenced? |

Map evidence to CQC's evidence categories where the current framework uses them:

- People's experience of health and care services.
- Feedback from staff and leaders.
- Feedback from partners.
- Observation.
- Processes.
- Outcomes.

Do not let the audit stop at "processes". For every domain, ask what outcome evidence exists. Examples: incident themes reduced, overdue policies closed, training compliance improved, cold-chain excursions corrected, complaints learning implemented, patient-access requests met, protocol gaps closed.

## Evidence Sources

Build an evidence registry before judging compliance.

| Evidence family | Examples to request or pull |
|---|---|
| Policies and SOPs | Master policies, SOPs, protocols, consent policy, safeguarding, IPC, medicines, BDD, GLP-1, emergency pathways. |
| Risk and COSHH | Risk register, risk assessments, COSHH inventory, SDS files, fire, legionella, lone working, sharps, equipment, patient suitability. |
| Registers and logs | Medicines register, fridge logs, defib logs, cleaning logs, fire log, legionella log, equipment register, incident book, complaints log. |
| Governance | Governance meeting agendas/minutes, action trackers, audit register, policy review tracker, risk register updates, annual reports. |
| Workforce | Training matrix, competency assessments, professional registrations, DBS/right-to-work checks, supervision/PDR records. |
| Patient care | Consent forms, Insync templates, consultation notes, aftercare sheets, complaint responses, incident disclosures, patient feedback. |
| Digital/CMS | Wix collections, website pages, public information, accessibility tools, booking forms, automated emails, data permissions. |
| External | Pharmacy agreements, waste contractor documents, calibration/service certificates, professional body evidence, insurance requirements. |

For each source record: title, owner, version/date, storage location, evidence type, related regulation, review date, and whether it was actually reviewed.

## Audit Mechanics From Neurodiversity

Apply these checks inside each domain:

### System map

For each domain, map:

- Inputs: patient requests, clinical data, stock, forms, staff actions, external alerts.
- Process: policy, SOP, protocol, risk assessment, role, timescale.
- Outputs: record, log, audit result, action, notification, patient information.
- Evidence store: Insync, Wix CMS, physical folder, governance minutes, register.
- Dependencies: linked documents, suppliers, pharmacy, external bodies, staff competence.
- Failure boundary: where harm, data breach, inspection failure, or legal exposure could occur.

### Load-bearing questions

Open one question at a time. Examples:

- Can the clinic prove that every treatment offered has a current protocol, consent pathway, risk assessment, emergency escalation, and staff competence evidence?
- Can the clinic trace each medicine from prescription/procurement to storage, administration, expiry/disposal, incident reporting, and audit?
- Can the clinic show that every patient-facing commitment has an operational owner, audit check, and learning loop?
- Can the clinic prove that reasonable adjustments requested by patients are recorded, actioned, and reviewed?

### Invariants

State the rules that must always remain true. Then search for a pathway or record that violates them.

Examples:

- No regulated treatment proceeds without valid consent recorded in Insync.
- No prescription-only medicine is administered without prescriber authorisation, batch/lot traceability, storage control, and expiry check.
- No active clinical protocol remains without a current linked risk assessment and review date.
- No COSHH substance is used without a current SDS and COSHH assessment.
- No patient-identifiable CMS collection is publicly readable.
- No incident closes without classification, duty-of-candour triage, action owner, and governance learning review.
- No high or extreme residual risk remains open without RM/NI escalation and risk-register entry.
- No patient access or communication adjustment request is ignored or left without a recorded response.

### Contradictions

Search for disagreement across:

`policy | SOP | protocol | form | register | log | training matrix | website | patient information | staff workflow | governance minutes | CMS permissions`.

Report contradictions as findings when they affect safety, consent, access, evidence, or inspection readiness.

### Negative space

Ask what should exist but does not. Common clinic gaps:

- A treatment is advertised but has no protocol, risk assessment, consent/aftercare sheet, complication pathway, or audit criterion.
- A policy makes a commitment but no form/log/register proves it.
- A risk assessment identifies a control but no SOP tells staff how to perform it.
- A log captures checks but has no escalation field for failed checks.
- A patient-facing page promises support but no internal SOP assigns ownership.
- A CMS collection exists but no owner or review cadence is documented.

### Failure modes

For each load-bearing domain, test:

- Missed check.
- Wrong patient.
- Wrong product/medicine.
- Expired product/equipment.
- Lost or inaccessible record.
- Permission exposure.
- Staff member absent or untrained.
- External supplier/pharmacy delay.
- Emergency pathway unavailable.
- Complaint or incident not escalated.
- Time pressure or sensory overload undermining consent comprehension.

For each confirmed failure mode, record severity, likelihood, detectability, controls, residual risk, and action.

## Scoring

### Assurance rating

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Very Good | Evidence is complete, current, owned, audited, outcome-linked, and improving. |
| Good to Very Good | Strong evidence with minor or low-risk gaps. |
| Good | Core compliance evidenced; targeted improvements needed. |
| Good with targeted improvement required | Generally controlled, but one or more material gaps need action. |
| Requires Improvement | Material evidence, governance, safety, access, or document-control gaps. |
| Inadequate | Serious or repeated failures, unsafe practice, absent ownership, or critical evidence missing. |

### Readiness severity

| Severity | Use when |
|---|---|
| Critical blocker | Immediate safety, safeguarding, medicines, data-protection, consent, legal, or inspection risk that must be addressed before the pathway continues. |
| Major | Material compliance gap likely to attract CQC concern or expose patients/staff to avoidable risk. |
| Moderate | Corrective action needed but controlled in the short term. |
| Minor | Housekeeping, clarity, or document-quality issue with limited risk. |
| Observation | Improvement opportunity or good-practice note. |

### Maturity score

| Score | Level | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Optimised | Complete, current, audited, outcome-linked, evidence-rich, improving. |
| 4 | Embedded | Strong evidence, clear ownership, minor gaps, stable improvement. |
| 3 | Established | Core system exists, but evidence depth or consistency needs work. |
| 2 | Developing | Material gaps in ownership, completeness, cadence, or action closure. |
| 1 | Initial | Sparse, stale, unowned, undocumented, or reactive. |

## Report Finding Format

Use this structure for each material finding:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Finding ID | Unique ID, e.g. CQC-SAFE-01. |
| Domain | Safe, Effective, Caring, Responsive, Well-Led, or cross-cutting. |
| Requirement | Regulation, standard, policy commitment, or recognised benchmark. |
| Evidence reviewed | Specific document/log/system/date, or "Evidence not supplied". |
| Finding | What was found, stated plainly. |
| Impact | Patient safety, governance, IG, equality/access, staff, or inspection effect. |
| Severity | Critical blocker, Major, Moderate, Minor, Observation. |
| Assurance impact | Effect on assurance rating or maturity score. |
| Required action | Concrete action with owner, timescale, and evidence of completion. |
| Verification | How closure will be proved. |

## Hostile Inspector Prompts

Use these before finalising:

- Show me the policy commitment.
- Show me how staff do it in practice.
- Show me the last completed record.
- Show me the audit that checks it.
- Show me what happened when the audit failed.
- Show me how the learning reached the Risk Register or Governance Committee.
- Show me staff competence evidence.
- Show me the patient information.
- Show me how the clinic adapts for people with communication, sensory, cognitive, mobility, or language needs.
- Show me the owner, review date, and version history.

If there is no answer, record a finding.
