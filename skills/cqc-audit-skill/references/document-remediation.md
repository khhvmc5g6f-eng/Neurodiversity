# Document Remediation

Use this reference when creating or amending policies, SOPs, protocols, risk assessments, COSHH assessments, forms, logs, checklists, registers, patient information, or governance templates after an audit.

## General Rules

- Preserve the original document. Create a revised version or a tracked change log rather than silently overwriting.
- Use formal UK English for internal governance documents.
- Use patient-friendly plain English for patient-facing documents and accessible summaries.
- Every document must state owner, approver, effective date, review date, review trigger, linked regulations, linked policies/SOPs, evidence produced, storage location, and audit mechanism.
- Every commitment must close a Plan/Do/Check/Act loop.
- Every gap identified in the audit must be traceable to either a new document, a revised document, an action-plan item, or a documented decision not to act with rationale.
- Every document should be capable of being produced during CQC inspection without further explanation.

## Amendment Workflow

1. Identify the source document, version, owner, date, and storage location.
2. Compare the document against the audit finding and the current live source.
3. Classify the amendment:
   - Critical safety correction.
   - Regulatory update.
   - Evidence-loop closure.
   - Version/document-control correction.
   - Cross-link correction.
   - Patient-facing accessibility/plain-language improvement.
   - Formatting or housekeeping.
4. Draft the amended version.
5. Add or update document control and enhancement log.
6. Update cross-linked domains and risk register/audit register references.
7. Add the change to the audit action tracker.
8. State what RM/NI, GAO, DSL, Prescriber, or other competent sign-off is needed.

## Policies

Policies must include:

- Metadata header: title, organisation, registered address, contact details, policy type, description, version, previous versions, effective date, review date, review frequency, owner, approver, CQC Provider ID, CQC key questions/quality statements, linked regulations, cross-linked domains, evidence examples, and benchmark sources.
- Enhancement log.
- Document control table.
- Full contents page.
- Purpose.
- Scope, including inclusions and explicit exclusions.
- Regulatory and governance framework.
- Definitions.
- Roles and responsibilities.
- Policy statement.
- Operational procedure or link to SOP.
- Risk assessment requirements.
- Consent, capacity, equality, accessibility, and safeguarding considerations.
- Clinical documentation requirements where relevant.
- Staff training and competence requirements.
- Incident reporting, complaints, Duty of Candour, MHRA Yellow Card, RIDDOR, or safeguarding escalation where relevant.
- Information governance and retention.
- Audit and monitoring framework.
- Risk register table.
- Compliance mapping table.
- Audit tool with binary criteria.
- Plan/Do/Check/Act loop table.
- CQC framework mapping.
- Cross-linked domains.
- References.
- Index for substantial policies.
- Final assurance statement.
- Panel or hostile-inspector review appendix for high-risk or CQC-facing documents.

## SOPs

SOPs must be operational, not essay-like. A staff member should be able to follow the SOP on a busy clinic day without needing verbal explanation.

Include:

- SOP title, reference code, version, owner, approver, effective date, review date.
- Purpose and scope.
- Who may perform the task and required competence/training.
- Equipment, medicines, products, forms, PPE, and records needed.
- Contraindications, stop criteria, escalation triggers, and explicit exclusions.
- Step-by-step procedure, one action per step.
- Checks before, during, and after the task.
- Documentation required, with where it is recorded and by whom.
- Failed-check procedure.
- Incident/near-miss reporting.
- Audit criterion and frequency.
- Linked policy, risk assessment, COSHH assessment, protocol, or form.

Avoid vague steps such as "check as appropriate" or "staff should be aware". Replace them with role, action, timescale, and evidence.

## Clinical Protocols

Clinical protocols must include:

- Treatment/procedure scope and indications.
- Eligibility criteria.
- Contraindications and deferral criteria.
- Consent requirements, including material risks and alternatives.
- BDD/psychological vulnerability screening where aesthetics is involved.
- Product/medicine details, batch/lot recording, storage requirements, expiry checks, and supplier controls.
- Prescribing model for prescription-only medicines.
- Pre-treatment assessment.
- Treatment steps.
- Complication recognition and emergency response.
- Aftercare and safety-netting.
- Follow-up and outcome monitoring.
- Patient record requirements.
- Staff competence.
- Audit criteria.
- Cross-links to policy, SOP, risk assessment, COSHH where relevant, and emergency pathway.

For GLP-1, botulinum toxin, dermal filler, skin boosters, peels, microneedling, PRP, polynucleotides, exosomes, phlebotomy, or medicines-related protocols, check live NICE/MHRA/SPC/BNF/professional guidance before finalising.

## Risk Assessments

Risk assessments must follow the Haus risk-assessment standard:

- Suitable and sufficient assessment under MHSWR 1999 Regulation 3.
- 5x5 likelihood x severity matrix.
- Inherent risk before controls.
- Existing controls.
- Additional controls required.
- Hierarchy of control in order: elimination, substitution, engineering, administrative, PPE.
- Residual risk after controls.
- ALARP reasoning, not merely assertion.
- Risk register entry carrying the highest residual risk.
- Owner, target date, review date, escalation threshold.
- Audit/monitoring tool.
- CQC/HSE framework mapping.
- Evidence pack checklist.

Any residual High or Extreme risk must be escalated to RM/NI and the Governance Committee. Extreme residual risk means the activity should stop or not start until reduced, unless a competent person documents a lawful emergency exception.

## COSHH Assessments

Each hazardous substance or clearly defined substance group must have:

- Product/substance name and use in this clinic.
- Supplier/manufacturer and current Safety Data Sheet version/date.
- COSHH reference code.
- Hazard classification and CLP/GHS pictograms where applicable.
- Routes of exposure: skin, eyes, inhalation, ingestion, injection, environmental.
- Persons at risk.
- Exposure scenario for actual clinic use.
- Workplace exposure limits where relevant.
- Storage, segregation, labelling, access control.
- Control measures through the hierarchy of control.
- PPE as the last line of control, not the main answer where higher controls are reasonably practicable.
- Spill procedure.
- First aid/emergency measures.
- Waste disposal route.
- Health surveillance trigger where relevant.
- Training and competence.
- Audit check and review trigger.
- Link to COSHH inventory and SDS library.

Common clinic substances to consider include disinfectants, hypochlorite, alcohol products, chemical-peel acids, hydrogen peroxide, sharps/clinical waste exposures, cleaning products, and any treatment products with hazardous handling requirements.

## Forms, Logs, Registers, Checklists, And Audit Tools

These are evidence generators. They should be easy to complete and hard to misinterpret.

Each tool must include:

- Title, version, owner, review date.
- Date/time field.
- Person completing.
- Patient identifier only where necessary and safe.
- Binary or objectively measurable checks.
- "Not applicable" options where appropriate.
- Failed-check field.
- Corrective action field.
- Escalation field.
- Sign-off field for the responsible role.
- Storage location and retention period.
- Audit frequency.

For daily/weekly logs, include a way to mark "clinic closed" so blanks are not confused with missed checks. For failed readings, require corrective action and escalation details.

## Patient-Facing Information

Patient-facing material must use plain English and support consent comprehension.

Include:

- What the treatment/service is.
- Who it is suitable for and who should not have it.
- Benefits, limits, alternatives, and option to decline.
- Material risks in understandable language.
- What will happen before, during, and after.
- How long it takes.
- What the patient needs to tell the clinic.
- What to do if something goes wrong.
- Contact routes and response times.
- Reasonable adjustments and support-person options.
- Data/privacy explanation where the material collects information.

Do not use patient-facing information as a substitute for clinician-led consent. It supports the conversation; it does not replace it.

## Panel Review For High-Risk Documents

For medicines, prescribing, injectables, safeguarding, consent/capacity, incidents/Duty of Candour, IPC, COSHH, fire, or any document likely to be central in a CQC inspection, run a documented panel-style review before finalising.

Use these perspectives:

- Legal/regulatory.
- Medical/clinical.
- CQC/HSE inspection.
- Governance/IG.
- Aesthetics compliance.
- Pharmacy/medicines safety/prescribing where relevant.
- Patient/service user.
- Neurodiversity and reasonable adjustments.
- Hostile inspector.

The review must produce findings, amendments made, remaining sign-off requirements, and cross-references to the sections that resolve the findings.

## Document Gap Matrix

Use this table in the audit report and remediation pack:

| Document | Exists? | Current? | Owner | Linked risk/protocol/policy | Evidence generated | Gap | Priority | Action | Owner | Due date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Priority should reflect patient-safety, legal/regulatory, data-protection, equality/access, and inspection exposure, not how easy the document is to write.
